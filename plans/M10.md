# M10 plan: the runtime in wrela

**Status: ACTIVE.**

First deliverable of the milestone, per the planning convention.
[ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md) M10 carries the substance — unusually for this
ladder, that section already settles the floor, the index rule, the item
order, two rejected alternatives, and the migration discipline. This plan
does not relitigate any of it. What it adds is the part ROADMAP names as
missing:

> **Two things to verify before this becomes plan text** (neither is
> established): that uniform turn areas are actually cheap for real
> images ... and that nothing else in the runtime holds a raw address —
> the group arena and the checkpoint stub's saved-register area have not
> been traced closely.

Both are measured below before any item is written.

## Where this milestone actually starts (measured, 2026-07-26)

Stated as numbers so the first item cannot begin by rediscovering them.

### Turn areas today, and what uniform stride costs

ROADMAP's first unverified prerequisite, measured over the whole golden
corpus (`wrela dump --stage=report` on every case, honouring `root`
markers; 103 images have at least one turn area, 51 have at least one real
async frame).

- A turn area is `TURN_RECORD_SIZE + widest owned async frame`, computed by
  identical arithmetic in three places: per actor
  (`layout.rs:5162-5174`), per messageable `@driver`
  (`layout.rs:5224-5234`), per free async fn (`layout.rs:5245-5249`).
  `TURN_RECORD_SIZE = 56` (`codegen.rs:5173`). Frame bytes come from
  `codegen::async_frame_sizes` (`codegen.rs:8089-8099`), which is
  word-granular — **observed areas are 8-aligned, not 16-aligned** (every
  measured size ≡ 8 mod 16).
- **Turn areas are not contiguous today.** `place_runtime_tables`
  (`layout.rs:5718-5775`) bump-allocates each actor's turn area
  immediately after that actor's own state/ring/head/tail/count. So item 0
  is *two* changes, not one: a stride change **and** a re-grouping into a
  single array. Budget the golden churn for both.
- Every turn area is already visible in the report (`Actor … frame=`,
  `Turn fn=… frame=`, `Driver … frame=`; `layout.rs:5354-5392`), which is
  what made this measurable at all.

Cost of `round_up_pow2(image-wide max)`:

| | multiplier | added bytes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| worst multiplier (`boot-handle-space-multi-kind`, 1144→6144) | ×5.37 | +5000 |
| worst absolute (`boot-init-args`, 2488→10240) | ×4.12 | **+7752** |
| median over all 103 turn-bearing images | ×1.14 | +16 |
| median over the 51 with a real async frame | ×2.98 | +1360 |

- Largest single frame anywhere in the corpus: **1576 bytes**
  (`boot-actor-reply-result`) → 2048 stride.
- **`n_turns` never exceeds 7** across the entire corpus.
- Guest DRAM ceiling is 1 GiB (`wrela-machine/src/lib.rs:68`), with 3 MiB
  already spent on per-core stacks alone (`lib.rs:88-92`). The worst added
  +7752 bytes is **0.00072% of the ceiling** and 0.25% of the stacks.
  Summed over all 103 images, uniform-pow2 adds 62.5 KB total.
- Plain 16-aligned uniform stride would be cheaper (median ×1.85, +592 B)
  but only by ~4 KB worst-case, and it costs the shifted-register-add that
  is the entire point (`add xN, base, idx, lsl #11` instead of a multiply).

**Verdict: adopt uniform `round_up_pow2`. The offset-table fallback is not
planned.** Padding cannot "multiply badly" while `n_turns` stays in single
digits — added bytes are bounded by `n_turns * stride` and both factors are
tiny. The alarming-looking ×5.37 multiplies a base measured in kilobytes.

**The one thing that would flip this** is `n_turns` growth: break-even
against a 64 KiB turn-array budget is ~**32 turns** at today's max stride,
and one pathological 4 KB-frame actor among 30 otherwise-bare turns would
cost 120 KB. That is a tripwire, not a re-plan — see decision 555.

Two caveats on the measurement, both recorded rather than hidden:
`boot-hello` and `boot-many-tests` have no `@image` fn so no report exists
for them (neither declares `@actor` or `async fn`, so neither can have a
non-trivial turn area, but this was not confirmed from a report); eight
further `err-`/no-`@image` cases could not be measured for the same reason.
Separately, `check-import-lower`'s `rtdata size=1138384` looked out of
family and was spot-checked by the orchestrator: it declares
`mailbox=SETTLED` where the imported const is 71142, so 71142 × 16 B is
correct and deliberate. Not a defect, but it is the corpus's proof that
**mailbox capacity is unbounded by anything today** — relevant to any
future runtime-table budget, not to turn stride.

**On the two measurements disagreeing.** Two independent recon passes
measured the same padding and reached opposite verdicts: one reported
"+7752 bytes worst, 0.00072% of the ceiling — adopt", the other "+46% to
+353% of rtdata — the padding multiplies badly, plan the offset table".
Both sets of numbers are correct; they differ only in denominator.
Adjudicated by the orchestrator in favour of the absolute reading, for
three reasons. (1) ROADMAP's criterion is "cheap for *real images*", and
the only real image in the corpus — `appliance` — is ×1.14/+16 bytes; the
alarming ratios come from micro-tests where one actor has a big async
frame and its siblings are bare 56-byte records, which is a statement
about test shape. (2) A ratio on a section measured in single-digit
kilobytes is not a cost; the quantity that has to be affordable is bytes,
and 7752 of them against 1 GiB is not a decision. (3) The offset table is
not free — it is one extra load on every waker dereference, forever, in
the hottest code in the image, bought to save bytes that do not exist.
Recorded here rather than in a decision because it is a reading of the
same evidence, not a new fact; decision 552 is where the choice lives.

### Raw addresses: seven, not one

ROADMAP's second unverified prerequisite. Both of its named unknowns
resolve **favourably**, and the surprises are elsewhere.

- **The group arena is already fully conformant.** Statically sized from
  `count_with_group_sites` (`layout.rs:4833`), placed at
  `place_runtime_tables:5793`, uniform 96-byte stride
  (`GROUP_SLOT_SIZE`, `codegen.rs:5266`), and every address computation
  into it is `arena_base + (index-1)*stride` from an index held in the
  frame's `Temp(0)`. `Reloc::GroupArenaBase` resolves the one base.
- **The checkpoint stub holds no runtime-varying reference at all.** There
  is no saved-register area: `build_checkpoint_and_vector_stub_ex`
  (`layout.rs:432-596`) saves exclusively SP-relative, and its
  preserve-`x28` contract is documented (`layout.rs:423-425`) and honoured
  by not touching the register, not by spilling it.

What the ROADMAP's one-line framing misses:

1. **`OFF_TURN_WAKER` is already a composite, not a bare address.** Since
   M8-C2 (decision 30) `build_rt_xsend` ORs `((src_core+1) << 61)` into it
   (`layout.rs:6012-6016`) and `.deliver` routes on the tag with an
   `lsr`/`bic`/compare chain (`layout.rs:6448-6472`). So a `TurnId` must
   either be **globally unique across cores** or become a pair. See
   decision 557.
2. **Six further raw addresses**, each crossing a turn boundary:
   cross-core reply-ring slot word 0 (`layout.rs:6089` → `6669`);
   `OFF_TURN_REPLY_SLOT`, an absolute frame-interior address
   (`codegen.rs:7141` → `layout.rs:6386`); group `join_waiter`
   (`codegen.rs:7222` → `layout.rs:6893`, dereferenced); group
   `owner_turn` (`codegen.rs:6169`, equality-compared only, so trivial);
   and **`SLOT_META_WAITER` / `SLOT_META_REPLY_STAGE`**
   (`codegen.rs:7259-7268` → `codegen.rs:2819-2839`), which live in
   **DMA-pool memory**, not `rtdata`.
3. `Receipt` is a raw meta address (`virtqueue.rs:147`) and is the only
   one that is a **language-visible value**. Out of scope — see non-goals.

### The BRK inventory is wrong in the ROADMAP, in both directions

There are **eight** live `BRK` constants, not five, and two of ROADMAP's
claims do not survive contact with the code:

- **`BRK_AWAIT_ACTOR_REJECTED` is already dead.** M7 item H3 converted the
  site to a legible abort (`codegen.rs:7156-7171`) that prints "await
  rejected: `<Actor>`'s mailbox was full". The constant is declared
  (`codegen.rs:5299`) and never emitted. Strike it from the list of traps
  M10 must dissolve.
- **`BRK_REPLY_SLOT_NO_WAKER` is not dissolved by a non-optional
  `TurnId`.** A genuine waker-less admission exists and is produced by
  `send`: `emit_marshal_and_call` writes `x3 = 0`
  (`codegen.rs:5793-5794`), `.deliver` branches on it (`cbz x11`,
  `layout.rs:6446`), and `emit_group_start` zeroes the field on a fresh
  child (`codegen.rs:6330`). Writing the non-optional type ROADMAP
  describes would produce a type that **cannot represent `send`**. See
  decision 558.
- **Two constants ROADMAP does not mention** — `BRK_XREPLY_RING_FULL`
  (`layout.rs:6057`) and `BRK_XREPLY_UNKNOWN_CORE` (`layout.rs:6483`) —
  join the inventory M10 dissolves; the first is an ordinary bounds check
  on a statically sized ring, the second is a no-arm-matched `match` that
  dissolves exactly when the core tag becomes a typed index.
- **`BRK_ACTOR_TURN_CANCELLED` is confirmed a representation gap**,
  verbatim in its own comment (`codegen.rs:5298-5300`): "the turn record
  has one scalar reply word and no error channel, so there is nothing
  honest to deliver to the awaiting turn". See decision 559.

### The hand-emitted inventory: 911 words, 20 floor

Measured per routine and cross-checked against the pinned `rtcode` section
sizes (471 words `boot-actors`, 501 `appliance`), so the formulas are
validated rather than asserted.

- **`boot-actors` as actually booted: 911 hand-emitted words (3,644
  bytes), 20 floor, 891 migratable — 97.8%.** `wrela build` images:
  `appliance` 577 words / 15 floor, `boot-actors` 552 / 18.
- The floor lands almost exactly on ROADMAP's "roughly twenty
  instructions", which is good evidence it was sized honestly. But **its
  composition is not the three categories ROADMAP names**: 5 words are
  category 1 (SP install), 5 are category 2 (the checkpoint save/restore),
  8 are category 3 `BRK`s, and **2 words are a `BR x9` computed indirect
  long-jump** (`push_abort_tail`, `layout.rs:8437-8445`) that fits none of
  them. See decision 650.
- Zero words are `eret`/`msr`/`mrs`/barriers — `encode.rs` has no encoder
  for any of them, exactly as ROADMAP predicts.
- **`build_fmt_dec` has zero floor words** and touches exactly one address.
  It is the cleanest migration target in the file, and confirms ROADMAP's
  choice of the formatter as item 2.
- **`build_ring_append` is not pure**, contrary to ROADMAP's framing: it
  read-modify-writes the persistent cursor `OFF_RING_DATA_BUMP` on every
  call and `BRK`s on overflow. The four console routines are a small state
  machine over three `machine_info` words, not four pure functions.
- **The `x30` bug is pinned only as a hang**, in a macOS-aarch64-only JIT
  suite, and **the same bug class already recurred once** in
  `build_boot_init` (`layout.rs:7344-7355`). A missing prologue save is
  structurally impossible in compiled code. That is the single strongest
  argument for this milestone and it should be quoted in its commits.

### `@layout(runtime)` and `@placed` are much larger than "a fourth variant"

The single most important correction in this plan, because ROADMAP frames
item 1 as nearly free ("not a new mechanism but a fourth layout class",
"`@placed`. One."). Measured against the code, neither holds.

- **Every field in ROADMAP's sketch is illegal under three independent
  existing rejections.** `[TurnArea; N_TURNS]` is `ast::Type::Array`,
  rejected at `sema/types.rs:1982-1990`; `TurnArea`/`Mailbox`/`GroupSlot`
  are nested `@layout` structs, rejected at `types.rs:2016-2025` (a gap
  whose own text attributes it to plans/M7.md item E, which never landed);
  `rr_cursor: usize` is rejected at `types.rs:1995-2005` as
  target-dependent. So the class needs **array fields with a const
  length**, **struct nesting with recursive offset computation**, and a
  `usize` answer.
- **The array length is the hard one.** `check_layouts` runs *before* name
  resolution (`sema/mod.rs:252-263`) and deliberately refuses to evaluate
  anything — `parse_offset_attr`'s own comment (`types.rs:1941-1945`)
  calls a `const`-named offset "inference by another name". Resolving
  `N_TURNS` inside that pass breaks its stated purity; moving the pass
  later breaks the two reasons its doc block gives for running early.
- **wrela has no statics at all.** `static` is not a keyword
  (`lexer.rs:87-92`), `ast::Item` has no `Static` variant
  (`ast.rs:87-94`), and 02-language.md never uses the word. `@placed` is
  one attribute *plus a new item kind*, with a mutability and aliasing
  story that has to answer to §3's ownership model, §4's pools, and actor
  turn isolation.
- **There is no fixed `RTDATA_BASE` to place against.** Every section base
  is a bump result (`layout.rs:2761-2776`); `rtdata` is at `0x40500bd8`,
  `0x40500938`, `0x40503380` in three different goldens. A fixed base
  means adding a constant to `wrela-machine`'s v1 map, which is
  **machine-revision-visible** (`lib.rs:21`, clause
  `machine.layout.v1-constants`).
- **`verify_section_sizes` does not already know how to check this.**
  Refuted by reading it (`layout.rs:1742-1789`): it takes
  `(sections, image_base, blob_len)`, never receives `RuntimeTables`,
  never names `rtdata`. Worse, its **gap rule** — adjacent sections must
  be less than 8 bytes apart (`layout.rs:1771-1779`) — is the concrete
  blocker: pinning a section to a fixed address in a bump-allocated image
  guarantees a larger gap, and that rule fires as `internal error:`, which
  the fuzz harness treats as a bug rather than an outcome.
- **Unknown attributes are silently ignored** (`sema/bodies.rs:1093-1098`
  says 02 §13's "unknown attributes are errors" rule "is not yet enforced
  anywhere"). So a doc-first commit would land a rule that nothing
  enforces and `@placed` would be dropped on the floor — a fail-open, in
  the milestone whose whole subject is removing fail-opens. See
  decision 561.

## Goal

The scheduler, mailbox admission, turn execution, group child polling, the
console formatter, and the abort path stop being hand-assembled A64 emitted
word-by-word from `layout.rs` and become **wrela source**, compiled by the
same backend as user code, dumped at every stage, and pinned by the same
goldens.

The point is not speed — ROADMAP is explicit that M10 will probably make
the scheduler *slower*. The point is that this code is currently exempt
from every oracle the project owns (no stage dump, no sema, no FlowWir, no
`diff-eval`, no mwir golden; its only oracle is a boot transcript), and
hand-assembly is not merely slow but **unimprovable** — no profile,
before/after, or regression lock can reach it. M10 makes it reachable.

## The differential oracle (measured, 2026-07-26)

M10's migration rule rests entirely on this set, so it is counted here
before any migration begins:

- **83 runtime-boot goldens** — golden cases carrying a `@test(runtime)`
  fn, whose `expected/test.txt` is a transcript produced by really booting
  the image on `wrela-vmm` under Hypervisor.framework. *(Corrected
  2026-07-26: the original count was 85; `grep -rl "@test(runtime)"
  tests/golden --include=input.wr` measures 83.)*
- **7 `xtask repro` replay lanes** — `repro_test_image`,
  `repro_choice_log_roundtrip`, `repro_replay_exit_code_contract`,
  `repro_deadline_cancel_replay_is_clock_log_driven`,
  `repro_cross_core_mailbox_depth_admissions`,
  `repro_cross_core_admission_replay`, `repro_blk_completion_replay`.
- **`xtask diff-eval`** — evaluator-vs-backend, smoke subset in `check`.
- **`bench guest`** — replay-based, exact counts (transcript bytes, exit
  code, vCPU exits, choice count) across every timed boot.

**The migration rule, restated so no item can soften it** (ROADMAP, "never
relax it"): a runtime routine's wrela version replaces its hand-assembled
version only after producing a byte-identical transcript on every existing
boot/replay golden. The hand-asm implementation is the reference oracle, so
it is deleted **after** the diff is clean, never before, and never in the
same commit that introduces its replacement. If a transcript differs and
the wrela version looks more correct, that is a finding to pin and escalate
in this plan — **not** a licence to re-bless the golden.

## Non-goals (M11+ territory, or permanently out)

- **Self-hosting the compiler.** Not on this ladder at all.
- **Touching codegen.** M10 is a client of the backend, not a change to it.
  The one exception ROADMAP itself names — a dense `match` over a
  comptime-known index becoming a jump table — is a *codegen* improvement
  benefiting every `match` in the language, and is explicitly deferred:
  M10 relocates the fix, it does not make it.
- **Optimizing the scheduler.** M10 makes the scheduler reachable by the
  cleverness budget; it does not spend it. The first optimization pays the
  full three-part price (profile, before/after on a named recording,
  regression lock) like everything else.
- **Bounds-check elision on `TurnId`.** M10 makes it *possible* (an index
  newtype whose only legal use is indexing `RT.turns`) and does not do it.
  When it happens it is a **proof**, the same shape as the existing erasure
  of impossible `CallError` variants (02 §9.4), never a fast path.
- **`@naked` + inline asm.** Rejected in ROADMAP with a full argument. Not
  relitigated here.
- **A fused global state machine** and **`WFE`/exclusive-monitor idle**.
  Both rejected in ROADMAP with full arguments. Not relitigated here.
- **`@brk` as language surface.** Rejected. The standing rule for this
  milestone: **a surviving explicit trap is a finding about the
  representation, not a case for an escape hatch.** `@brk` may be added
  only per-site, with that argument made and rejected in writing first.

## Numbered decisions

M10 decisions start at **552** (M9 ended at 551 — item RR was renumbered
from 540–546 on 2026-07-26, because item K1 had already taken 540–544 and
landed first; that collision is exactly what this file's reservation scheme
exists to prevent, and it happened in M9 because M9 had no scheme).

Blocks are reserved per work stream so parallel agents cannot collide:
**552–569 orchestrator** (552–569 spent), **570–579 item 0**, **580–589
items A/A2/A2b/A2c/A2d** (580–589 spent; 582–585 A2d),
**590–599 item B**, **600–609 item C**, **610–619 item D**, **620–629
item E**, **630–649 items F/F2**, **650–669 orchestrator overflow**
(650–660 spent; 661–664 bench lock; 665–666 reply tag; **667** A2d
Input-omit; 668–669 spare), **670–689 items
G–I**, **690–699** sweep and close, **700–709** the fault-message fix
(spent), **710–719** item A3. **Every agent brief states its block**; an
agent that needs more numbers asks rather than borrowing.

**552. Uniform turn areas use `round_up_pow2(image-wide max)`.** Measured
above: worst case +7752 bytes, 0.00072% of the 1 GiB ceiling, with
`n_turns ≤ 7` corpus-wide. Rejected: the `[u64; N_TURNS]` offset-table
fallback ROADMAP offers — it costs one extra load per waker dereference
forever to save bytes that do not exist at this scale. Also rejected: plain
16-byte-aligned uniform stride, which is ~4 KB cheaper worst-case and
gives up the shifted-register-add (`add xN, base, idx, lsl #k`) that is the
whole reason for uniformity.

**553. The stride is image-wide, not per-core.** *(Premise corrected
2026-07-26 by item 0's design pass: this decision claimed `stripe_for_cores`
"restripes `rtdata` per core today". It does not. It is at `layout.rs:3740`,
not 5690, and it stripes only `ready_queue_capacity * 8 + RR_CURSOR_SIZE` —
turn areas have never been per-core. The conclusion below is unchanged and
now trivially true; `stripe_for_cores` needs no edit in item 0.)*
`stripe_for_cores` restripes the scheduler stripe per core today. One stride for
the whole image keeps `TurnId` a single flat index with no core component,
which is what makes a cross-core waker the same value as a local one.
Rejected: per-core strides, which would make `TurnId` a pair and reintroduce
exactly the address-shaped reasoning item 0 exists to remove.

**554. Item 0 re-groups turn areas into one contiguous array.** Not merely
a stride change: `place_runtime_tables` interleaves each turn area with its
actor's state and ring today. `RT.turns` must be one array for `TurnId` to
index it. This is disclosed here because it doubles item 0's golden churn
(every `report.txt` base changes), and a surprise of that size mid-item is
how a "pure representation refactor" stops being verifiable.

**555. The turn array's size becomes a reported, assertable fact.** The
measurement shows uniform stride is free *at today's scale* and names the
scale at which it stops being free (~32 turns). Rather than re-plan for
that, item 0 publishes `turn_stride` and total turn-array bytes in the
report so an image can `@layout_assert` its own ceiling — the tripwire the
measurement asks for, using machinery that already exists. Rejected:
hardcoding a compiler-enforced limit, which invents a policy no doc states.

**556. Uniform stride lands byte-identical or it does not land.** Item 0 is
a pure representation refactor in Rust with no language change in flight,
so every one of the 83 runtime-boot goldens and 7 replay lanes must produce
an identical *transcript*. Section bases and `rtdata` sizes in `report.txt`
**will** change and are expected to; transcripts, exit codes, vCPU exit
counts and choice counts must not. That split — report may move, behaviour
may not — is the acceptance test for item 0.

*Exception, found by item 0a and stated rather than waived (2026-07-26).*
Exactly one transcript in the corpus **quotes a guest address**:
`err-boot-blk-cross-device-pool`'s fault message prints the offending range
(`[0x405042e0, +512)`). Since this decision already licenses `rtdata` to
change size and therefore every later section to move, that address must
move too, and the "no transcript changes" rule as written is impossible to
satisfy. The rule is therefore: **a transcript may change only where it
quotes an address that moved for a reason this commit already licenses, and
only when the change is verifiable as a pure rebase** — same object, same
offset within it, shift equal to the licensed growth. Verified by the
orchestrator for this case: `pooldata` shifted `0x550`, the quoted address
shifted `0x550`, the offset **within the `pooldata` section** is `0xa30`
before and after, and `rtdata` grew by exactly `1360 = 0x550`. *(Corrected
2026-07-26: that `0xa30` was first written here as the offset within
`BlockPayloads`, which is wrong — `0x405038b0` is the base of the
`pooldata` section, not of the pool. The pool-relative offset is `0x0`,
because `BlockPayloads` is a single 512-byte slot and the refused range is
the whole of it. The rebase proof is unaffected: it only ever needed the
address and the section to move by the same amount, which they did.)* Anything that does not
decompose that way is a behaviour change and falls back under ROADMAP's
"not a licence to re-bless the golden".

This is a narrow exception and it should stay narrow: it is available only
to items 0a/0b, whose whole subject is moving bytes. **No migration item
(B through I) may invoke it** — a migrated routine's transcript differing
is the thing the differential oracle exists to catch.

**557. `TurnId` is one flat image-wide index; the core tag stays a separate
field — specifically, two adjacent `u32` halves of the existing word, never
a second word.** The waker today packs `(core+1) << 61` with a 61-bit
address. Item 0 splits that one 64-bit word into `waker_turn: u32`
(`Option[TurnId]`) at +32 and `waker_core: u32` (`Option[CoreId]`) at +36.

*Sharpened 2026-07-26 by item 0's design pass, which found the loose reading
is expensive.* "Separate adjacent field" read as a separate 64-bit **word**
costs +8 bytes on every mailbox slot **and** every cross-core request-ring
slot image-wide, because the tag rides the whole
`xsend → ring → mailbox → turn record` chain and the waker lives *inside*
the mailbox slot (`ActorRuntimeLayout::slot_size` = 16 + widest param blob).
On `check-import-lower`, whose `mailbox=SETTLED` is 71142, that is
**+556 KiB** — about nine times the entire corpus-wide cost of uniform
stride, to buy nothing. The two-`u32` encoding costs **zero** bytes, keeps
`TURN_RECORD_SIZE = 56`, `slot_size` and `REPLY_SLOT_SIZE = 16` unchanged,
and is still two typed fields addressed with `ldr w`/`str w` — no shift, no
mask, no `bic`. Any future reader tempted to "tidy" this into one packed
word is reintroducing the bit-twiddling the milestone exists to delete. Rejected:
a packed `(core, index)` pair, which reproduces in a new encoding exactly
the bit-twiddling the milestone exists to delete, and would make
`BRK_XREPLY_UNKNOWN_CORE` survive as a decode failure. Rejected also:
making `TurnId` per-core, which would make a cross-core waker a different
type from a local one — see decision 553.

**558. The waker is `Option[TurnId]`, and the trap it dissolves is
dissolved by typing the admission, not by making the field non-optional.**
`send` produces a genuinely waker-less admission, so a non-optional field
cannot represent the machine. What actually makes
`BRK_REPLY_SLOT_NO_WAKER` unreachable is the invariant already stated in
its own doc comment (`layout.rs:6200-6208`): the guard sits only in an arm
whose method's declared reply is an aggregate, and `check_send_call` forces
`send` targets to be unit-returning, so no aggregate-reply method can be
admitted waker-less. Item 0 therefore records that invariant as a **typed
per-method-color admission** and the trap goes with it. This corrects
ROADMAP's stated argument; the conclusion (the trap dies) is unchanged.

**559. `BRK_ACTOR_TURN_CANCELLED` is answered by a reply tag, and the shape
already exists in the machine.** The turn record carries `reply` (+24, one
scalar) and `reply_slot` (+48, a staging pointer) and no tag, which is why
cancellation has nothing honest to deliver. The group arena already stores
its children as `(tag, payload)` pairs
(`group_child_tag_off`/`group_child_payload_off`, `codegen.rs:5281-5286`).
One extra `reply_tag` word in the turn record, written by
`.deliver`/`rt_drain`/`rt_xreply` and read by `emit_await_resume`, makes
`Ok`/`Cancelled`/`NotAdmitted` three deliverable values instead of one
scalar and two traps. **Scheduled as item J, not item 0** — it is a
behaviour change, and item 0's whole value is being behaviour-preserving.

**560. `SLOT_META_WAITER`/`SLOT_META_REPLY_STAGE` are in scope for the
`TurnId` change, which forces a cross-layout-class decision.** These two
turn-area references live in DMA-pool memory, so `TurnId` must be a legal
field type inside a `@layout(dma)` struct. Decided now rather than during
migration: **`TurnId` is a `u32` newtype** whose representation in any
layout class is a plain `u32`, so it is legal wherever `u32` is and
introduces no new field-type rule. Rejected: leaving these two as raw
addresses, which would leave the index rule with a documented exception in
its first milestone — the rule's value is that it has none.

**561. The doc-first commit lands a fail-closed rejection in the same
commit, not just prose.** ROADMAP requires the `@layout(runtime)`/`@placed`
doc and ledger change before any migration. But unknown attributes are
silently ignored today, so prose alone would land a normative rule that
nothing enforces — a fail-open, in the milestone about removing fail-opens.
Item A therefore ships the doc, the clauses (as `gap`), **and** a named
`error[unimplemented]` for `@placed` and for `@layout(runtime)` until their
implementing items land. Rejected: deferring enforcement to the
implementing item, which is how a documented-but-ignored attribute becomes
permanent.

**562. `@layout(runtime)`'s new field-type rules are their own item,
sequenced before the class exists.** Array fields with a const length,
nested layout structs, and the `usize` question are three separate
capabilities in `layout_field_size`/`check_one_layout`, and the const
length collides with `check_layouts` running before name resolution. This
is the "item balloons" case GOAL.md names: split visibly rather than
discover it mid-item. Item A is doc+ledger+fail-closed; **item A2** is the
field-type work; the class is not usable until A2 lands.

**563. `rr_cursor` is `u64`, not `usize`.** The cheapest answer to the
`usize`-in-a-layout rejection, and the correct one: `@layout` types are
exact-bytes and `usize` is target-dependent by definition, which is
precisely why the existing rule rejects it. Rejected: exempting `usize`
inside `runtime` layouts, which would make one layout class
target-dependent and break the property the whole mechanism exists for.

**564. Ledger ids split on doc file, not on milestone.** ROADMAP says M10
opens `runtime.*` clauses. But `@layout(runtime)` and `@placed` are
03-hardware.md §3 rules, and every existing 03 clause is `hardware.*`.
Layout-class rules therefore open as `hardware.layout.*` beside their three
siblings, and `runtime.*` is reserved for M10's actual subject — the
scheduler/mailbox/turn machinery becoming wrela. Recorded as a deviation
from ROADMAP's wording, not from its intent.

**565. Two of the seven references are `(TurnId, offset)` pairs, not bare
indices — and this plan said otherwise.** `OFF_TURN_REPLY_SLOT`
(`codegen.rs:7141` → `layout.rs:6442`) and `SLOT_META_REPLY_STAGE`
(`codegen.rs:7253` → `codegen.rs:2819`) store *frame-interior* addresses
whose intra-area offset is `Frame::reply_stage_off`, assigned sequentially
per fn (`codegen.rs:739-743`), with the reader in a different fn from the
writer. No `TurnId` alone recovers them. The "Raw addresses" section above
even calls the first "an absolute frame-interior address" and then lists it
as becoming an index; that was a contradiction, found by the design pass
rather than by the implementation, which is where it was supposed to be
found. **Item 0 stores each as two adjacent `u32`s** — `(TurnId, byte
offset within that turn area)` — at zero byte cost. Rejected for item 0: a
fixed-offset reply-stage region, which is the real end state but requires
moving `reply_stage_off` out of the sequential frame and re-basing
`slot_bias`, touching every async frame offset in every asm golden — a
frame-layout change wearing a representation-refactor costume. That belongs
to item A2/E, and the exit criterion's "a grep finds indices" is worded to
accept an index-plus-offset pair.

**566. The core tag may be removable entirely, but not in item 0.** A
turn's owning core is a build-time fact (`reply_ring_capacity`,
`layout.rs:1304-1312`, already computes exactly that partition; free turns
are core 0 by construction). With a flat image-wide `TurnId`, `.deliver`
could route by a compare chain over `TurnId` — `n_turns ≤ 7` corpus-wide —
carrying no core anywhere and dissolving `BRK_XREPLY_UNKNOWN_CORE` early.
Recorded so item F does not re-derive it. **Not taken in item 0** because it
changes `.deliver`'s control-flow shape rather than only its data, and item
F is exactly "a dense match over a comptime-known index".

**567. `TurnId` is 1-based; 0 is the `None` niche, deliberately.** Array
index 0 is a live turn in every actor-bearing image, so the niche is a
choice, not a gift — decision 558's "0 already serving as the niche" was
true only by accident. `TurnId(array_index + 1)`. Three reasons: it is
already the machine's convention for this exact problem (group ids are
`arena_index + 1` with 0 = no ambient group); it keeps all nine existing
zero tests (`cbz`/`str xzr`) instruction-identical; and the alternative —
reserving slot 0 as dead padding — costs a full stride and makes the
reported turn count off by one from the number of real turns, which is
precisely the quiet lie decision 555's reported fact exists to prevent.
`TurnId(0)` must be unconstructible in Rust.

**568. Item 0 publishes the `Turns` report line; making it
`@layout_assert`-able is a separate item.** Decision 555 said "machinery
that already exists". Half true: the report line is free, but `ImageReport`
(`stdlib/core/image_report.wr`) is a **closed eight-field surface** enforced
exactly-both-ways by `eval/layout_assert.rs:117-153`, is normative in
02-language.md §12.1, and today's synthesizer never reads `layout.runtime`.
Exposing `turn_stride`/`turns_bytes` to an assertion needs two stdlib
fields, two `REPORT_FIELDS` entries, two `ReportField` variants, a ledger
clause and a REVIEW-QUEUE line. **Do not absorb a stdlib surface change
into a byte-identity commit.**

**569. Item 0 splits into five commits, not three.** 0a stride (three
arithmetic expressions, no `TurnId`, no ABI change, no new reloc); 0b
re-grouping plus the `Turns` line; then 0c1 the waker chain, 0c2 the group
pair, 0c3 the DMA pair — each a closed set of writer/reader pairs sharing
no word with the others, which is the property that makes a byte-identity
failure bisectable to one field. The acceptance check for 0a is mechanical
and worth stating: **every `Actor …/Turn fn=…/Driver … frame=` line in
every report is unchanged**, because the per-owner raw area stays the
per-owner raw area and only `total_bytes` and placement switch to the
stride. Overwriting `frame_size` with the stride would churn ~100 report
lines and destroy the information a reviewer needs to see the padding.


**580. `@layout(runtime)` array lengths are integer literals (item A2).**
`check_layouts` runs before name resolution and `parse_offset_attr`
already refuses a `const`-named offset as "inference by another name"; an
array length is the same question, so accepting one while refusing the
other would be two answers to one rule. Rejected: scanning the module for
`const N = <literal>`, which is a second name resolver that can disagree
with the real one on imports, shadowing and `comptime if`-guarded consts —
a hazard `sema/types.rs` already carries a note ruling out.

**581. That literal-only bound blocks the milestone's central mechanism,
so item A2b lifts it with a second pass.** A2's own report named the
consequence and it is decisive: the field *types* now exist but the
*lengths* do not. M10's tables are sized per image — `n_turns`,
`reply_ring_capacity`, the group arena's capacity — so a literal length
cannot spell any of them, and ROADMAP's whole `@layout(runtime)` design is
"the whole `rtdata` section is one typed static" with `[TurnArea;
N_TURNS]`. Verified: the compiler refuses 03 §3.1's own example verbatim.
That is a docs-versus-code disagreement, and by ground-truth rule 1 the
code is wrong.

**A2b therefore adds a second, later layout-completion pass** that re-runs
sizing after const evaluation, leaving the early pass exactly as pure as
decision 580 requires. Both reasons the early pass runs early are
preserved: it still checks shape before name resolution, so
`err-layout-wire-capability` keeps its better diagnostic. Rejected:
changing 03 §3.1's example to a literal, which would keep the compiler
honest by making the milestone impossible; and relaxing the early pass,
which decision 580 already settled.

**590. ROADMAP's console-`BRK` dissolution claim is false, and the failure
mode it promises is inverted.** ROADMAP says
`BRK_LINE_APPEND_OVERFLOW`/`BRK_LINE_COMMIT_OVERFLOW` "become ordinary
array-bounds checks on the fixed-size console ring, which codegen already
emits everywhere — better than today, since a bounds failure gains a
diagnostic instead of a bare trap." Measured: a bounds failure emits 25
words ending in `bl <__wrela_abort_val>`, and `__wrela_abort_val`'s body is
four calls into `__wrela_ring_append` plus one into `__wrela_fmt_dec` — **the
diagnostic is produced by the routine whose bounds check just failed.**
The recursion has no depth bound (`push_abort_tail`'s `BR x9` is only
reached after the printing) and **does not fault**: the VMM maps the whole
1 GiB RW in one `hv_vm_map`, so the runaway stack walks down through
`console::DATA_BASE` — the transcript itself — and the machine-info page,
corrupting `OFF_EXIT_CODE` and `OFF_TEST_CONTINUATION` before reaching
unmapped memory. Today's `BRK` is decoded and named by the VMM. So the
change would be strictly worse. **Correct the ROADMAP text** and treat this
as one of the three examples in its `@brk` paragraph being wrong.

**591. Decision 652's acceptance criterion is unsatisfiable as written and
becomes a reachability criterion plus a structural guard.** "No
`Reloc::AbortFixed`/`AbortVal` in the emitted code of any console routine"
cannot hold: every source-level index emits one, and elision is an explicit
M10 non-goal. Replaced by: *no **reachable** abort edge — every failure the
routine can itself detect is returned as a value, and the abort path
carries a one-word re-entrancy latch that routes a second entry straight to
the existing halt tail, with a golden pinning that the latch is never set
on any green boot.* That makes the cycle **structurally impossible** rather
than argued unreachable, which is the trade this project already makes.
`@no_abort` is rejected again, but on the correct ground: decision 652's
stated reasoning (wrapping arithmetic dissolves the cycle for free) does
**not** transfer to bounds, since nothing dissolves a bounds check for
free — it is rejected because it removes the handling of a failure the
compiler just proved it could not rule out.

**592. The two overflow guards become status returns.** `check_transcript_bound`
already proves at build time that no line the driver composes exceeds
either bound, so no golden reaches them and no transcript can move.
Strictly better than a bare `BRK`, and it takes floor category 3 from 8
words to 6.

**593. Item B is blocked on three capabilities no item owns, so B0 is
promoted to its own item (A2c).** A/A2/A2b landed the doc, field sizing and
const lengths — a `@layout(runtime)` **type**. There is no `static` item
kind, `@placed` is refused in every attribute position by design
(`golden/err-placed-unimplemented`), and there is **no codegen path for a
`@layout(runtime)` value**. A type with no instance cannot be addressed, so
item B cannot write one line. The plan's dependency line "`B`…`F2` … each
depends on `A2`" is insufficient for every one of B through F2. Land it
against the three **machine pages** (`MACHINE_INFO_BASE`,
`console::RING_BASE`, `console::DATA_BASE`), which are outside the image
blob and already in `wrela-machine`'s v1 map — so neither named `@placed`
risk fires: `verify_section_sizes`' 8-byte gap rule applies to *sections*,
and no machine-revision bump is needed. That makes item B's neighbourhood
the cheapest place in the milestone to land the mechanism.

**594. wrela cannot represent a packed byte buffer today, except through
`@layout`.** Measured: `[u8; 64]` occupies **512 bytes** — `mwir::size_of`
gives every scalar an 8-byte slot and an array is `elem * n`, so a `u8`
element is stored with a 64-bit `str`. `Bytes[N]` and `String[..N]` are the
same slot-per-byte shape by design and in writing. Only `@layout` fields
are dense. The console data region is packed transcript bytes the VMM
reads, so **`@layout(runtime)` is not a convenience for item B, it is the
only viable representation.**

**595. A wrela fn cannot receive, index or read image literal bytes today,
and `ring_append`'s `x0` is a second raw pointer decision 654 did not
name.** Four measured refusals: `Static[Str]` and `Static[Bytes[N]]` values
are `error[unimplemented]`, `Static[Bytes[N]]` cannot be indexed, and a
`b"..."` literal does not coerce to a `Bytes[N]` parameter. Yet copying
literal bytes into the ring is `ring_append`'s entire job. D0's fix for
`args_ptr` — make the value arrive by value — does not apply to N bytes of
rodata. **Resolution: split the routine.** `console_append_line_buf(len)`
has no pointer at all; `console_append_bytes(src: Bytes, len)` takes an
**unbounded `Bytes` parameter**, which 02 §6.2 *already specifies*
("A `read` or `mut` parameter of a bounded type may omit the bound") and
which only codegen refuses. Implemented as a two-word (base, len) handle —
the same discipline `Mmio[L]` already uses, where the handle is a plain
`u64` base in a register and source cannot observe the address. Rejected:
per-`N` monomorphization, on measured grounds — the appended literals
include `"test <name>: "`, one distinct length per distinct test-name
length, so instantiations grow with the corpus.

**596. `Bytes` has two byte representations and item B must pick one.**
Packed in rodata (`append_rodata`), slot-per-byte as a value
(`mwir::size_of`, where the dense alternative is recorded as rejected).
Unnoticed only because `Static[Bytes[N]]` values are unimplemented.
**Packed is correct** — it is what the device, the VMM and rodata all use —
which makes slot-per-byte `Bytes[N]` the anomaly to reconcile later. A
numbered decision rather than a side effect of the first item to pass image
bytes into wrela.

**597. Every compiled loop back-edge makes console output a scheduler
dispatch point.** `FnCtx::checkpoint` splices 6 words plus a conditional
`bl <__wrela_checkpoint_service>` onto every back-edge, and that service is
the real mask-arm-recheck loop that dispatches vector 0 — the deadline and
cancel scan that force-resumes turns. A wrela `ring_append` has a byte-copy
loop, so a nested dispatch could print mid-line against the global
`OFF_LINE_START`, and worse inside a `noreturn` abort that has already
committed to a landing pad. Impossible today because the hand-asm has no
checkpoints. **Design it out rather than test for it:** write the copy
without a back-edge (a bounded unrolled copy has none). Rejected:
exempting the runtime module from checkpoint emission, which is a codegen
change and an explicit non-goal. Affects B, C, E, F, F2, G, I.

**598. The migration costs an order of magnitude in code size, and the
number belongs here before item B starts.** Measured: `build_fmt_dec` is 52
hand-emitted words; the equivalent on the existing Format contract is
**480 words**, and one two-value f-string line in a real golden is 2708.
ROADMAP warns the scheduler gets slower; nobody warned that `code` grows
~9x per migrated routine. Not a reason to stop — it is the trade — but it
must be a known number next to the bench lock, not a discovery.

**599. Decision 655 removes the `wrela build` abort stubs without noting
that `check_transcript_bound` never runs there.** It is called only from
`layout_test_image`. So after 655, a `wrela build` image's overflow guard
has no build-time proof behind it: either extend the bound check to
`layout_program`, or that overflow is genuinely reachable and must be a
value rather than an assertion. Item B decides it, because item B's
status-return design is what makes the answer available.

**610. Item D passes message arguments by value in `x1`/`x2`; `nargs`
leaves the ABI.** Decision 654 framed (a) by-value-in-registers and (b) a
fixed-size array parameter as genuine alternatives. Measured, they are not:
**an array parameter in wrela is a by-pointer aggregate.** Verified by the
orchestrator — `fn consume(a: [u64; 3])` compiles to `ldr x9,[x0,#0]` in the
callee prologue, `is_aggregate` classifies `Type::Array` as by-pointer
(`codegen.rs:607`), and each constant-index read is *bounds-checked* into a
`bl __wrela_abort_val` (three of them, for three indices). So (b) does not
remove the pointer; it moves it to `x0` and adds an `N`-word prologue copy
plus a `noreturn` call per element. Two further measured facts settle it:
181 of 181 call sites pass 0, 1 or 2 words (117/60/4), and **the consumer
half is already by-value in `x1`/`x2`** (`layout.rs:6391-6397`), so this
makes the two halves symmetric rather than inventing a convention. `nargs`
leaves the ABI because the routine is per-actor specialized, which makes
the count a build-time constant of the generated routine.

**611. `TurnId` and `CoreId` are by-value at the ABI boundary, which
requires naming them in codegen's builtin list.** A blocking constraint
decision 557 did not state. `Option[T]` is `SLOT + size_of(T)` with no
niche packing (`mwir.rs:970`) and is passed **by pointer** — verified:
`fn consume(w: Option[u32], c: u64)` loads two words through `x0`. A
one-field `struct TurnId` is `Type::Named(..)` and by-pointer too
(`codegen.rs:607`). The *memory* encoding (two adjacent `u32`s, decision
557) is fine and unaffected; the *parameter* encoding is not.
`TurnId`/`CoreId` therefore join the by-value builtin list at
`codegen.rs:590-605`, where `Actor`, `Group`, `Instant`, `Duration` and
`InterruptCell` already live — a small addition with direct precedent.
Rejected: passing a bare `u32` and reading decision 567's niche by hand at
every site, which throws away the type the milestone exists to introduce.

**612. Item D splits: D0 flips the convention with Rust on both sides,
then D replaces the body.** Decision 653 said item 0 and item D "land
together" because both move `x3`. Finer granularity, same intent. **D0**
changes `emit_marshal_and_call` to pass by value, replaces
`build_ring_enqueue`'s copy loop with `min(cap,2)` conditional stores,
teaches `build_rt_drain` to load `x1`/`x2` from the ring slot, and deletes
the now-dead 2-word `arg_scratch` from every FlowWir frame
(`codegen.rs:5459-5495`) — all with hand-asm on both sides, so it is a
value/convention change exactly as 653 requires. **D0 lands before item 0**,
so the 16-byte frame shrink is absorbed by the stride and report churn item
0 has already licensed rather than moving every `frame=` line a second
time. Then D replaces the body against an ABI with no pointer left in it.

*Consequence, recorded because it costs rework:* item 0a was already
implemented and verified when this decision was taken. D0 shrinks every
async frame by 16 bytes, which changes the maximum that feeds
`round_up_pow2` — `boot-handle-space-multi-kind`'s widest area goes
1032 → 1016 and its stride 2048 → 1024, halving that image's turn array.
So 0a's *code* is unaffected but its pinned numbers are not. 0a is held,
D0 lands first, and 0a is rebased and its expectations regenerated. Doing
it the other way would move every `frame=` line and every stride twice,
and would make 0a's own acceptance check — 'every `frame=` line
unchanged' — read as though it had been wrong.

**710. The corpus-sema tables key on block content, not line number.**
Both `corpus_sema_context` (declaration preambles) and
`corpus_sema_census` (pinned accepted disagreements) keyed a doc block by
absolute line number, so any insertion above a fence silently detached its
preamble — item A hit exactly this and produced three disagreements
unrelated to its edit. Now the first 12 hex of sha256 over the fence body,
reusing the compiler's own `sha256_hex` rather than adding a dependency.
Rejected: keying by fence *ordinal* within the doc, which survives prose
insertions but not the insertion of a new fence, and silently remaps
rather than failing.

**711. Losing a pin when a block's body is edited is correct, not a
regression.** A content key changes when the content changes, so the
harness loses the pin and says so. That is the fail-closed direction: a
changed block's pinned classification genuinely does need re-review. The
diagnostics therefore have to be *good* — they name the doc, the current
line, the old and new keys, and the file to edit. Two of the three guards
are new coverage rather than a port: keys must be unique (two
byte-identical fences would make a pin ambiguous, so it refuses rather
than choosing), and every stub must attach to some live block — the silent
detach that started this was previously not an error at all.

**712. `corpus --sema` prints the key for every row.** Not only for
disagreements: `ok` rows are hand-pinned too, and a key the report does
not print is a key nobody can pin.

**650. The floor has a fourth category: a stored code address, jumped to.**
`push_abort_tail` loads `OFF_TEST_CONTINUATION` and `BR x9`s to a landing
pad across arbitrarily many un-popped frames; `build_entry_driver`
materializes its own future code address into that slot
(`layout.rs:8870-8871`). wrela has no `goto`, no `longjmp`, and no function
pointers, and M10 is not adding any. Recorded as **floor category 4** —
2 words — rather than left unclassified. Rejected: adding function
pointers, which is a language surface far larger than `@naked`, itself
already rejected for relocating twenty words.

**651. Item B is all four console routines, not the two ROADMAP names.**
`build_line_begin` (11 words) and `build_line_commit` (60) share the three
`machine_info` cursor words with the two named routines, and `line_commit`
is the largest of the four. Migrating two of four would leave the console
formatter half in wrela and the harder half — the virtio descriptor write
with mixed 64/32-bit fields — in Rust, which is not "proves the toolchain
end to end". Item B is 154 words, 2 floor `BRK`s.

**652. Runtime code uses the wrapping arithmetic family, never the checked
family — every migration item, not just B and C.** The blocker R3 found and the reason it is a decision rather than
a discovery: checked `+ - *` abandon on overflow (02 §6.1), codegen lowers
abandon to `BL __wrela_abort`, and `__wrela_abort` **is** the console
formatter. A wrela console formatter written with checked arithmetic calls
itself on any overflow check. So every arithmetic operation in **every**
migration item uses `+% -% *%`. *(Scope widened 2026-07-26 by item D's
design pass: `rt_enqueue`'s `tail + 1` and `count + 1` would emit
`Reloc::AbortFixed` under checked arithmetic, making admission non-leaf
and coupling the mailbox to the abort path. The rule was written for the
console formatter and is not special to it.)* Rejected: a `@no_abort` attribute, which is a new
language surface bought to paper over a cycle that wrapping arithmetic —
already in the language, already correct for these routines, since every
one of them is doing modular cursor arithmetic — dissolves for free.
**This constraint is load-bearing and easy to violate silently**, so item
B's acceptance includes: no `Reloc::AbortFixed`/`AbortVal` in the emitted
code of any console routine.

**653. Item 0 and item D land together.** The `rt_enqueue` ABI fixes
`x3 = waker`, and item 0 changes the waker from an address to a `TurnId`.
That is the same ABI, so the conformance step for item 0's waker change and
item D's migration cannot be separated without leaving one commit where the
ABI's two sides disagree. Sequenced as: item 0 changes the *value* with the
hand-asm on both sides (byte-identical transcripts), item D changes the
*implementation*. Both cite the ABI note in
`actors.calls.callerror-composition`.

**654. `args_ptr` is the one ABI element that cannot survive translation
unchanged.** `emit_marshal_and_call` (`codegen.rs:5766-5799`) passes
`x1` = the address of a caller frame slot and `x2` = a word count; wrela
has no pointer type and M10 is not adding one. Item D must therefore either
(a) change the marshalling convention so arguments arrive by value in
registers for the ≤2-word cases the corpus actually uses, or (b) express
the copy as a fixed-size array parameter. Decide in item D, record which,
and pin the rejected one. This is the largest single translation risk in
the milestone and it is named here so item D does not meet it as a
surprise.

**655. `build_entry_stub` and the two `build_abort_stub`s are deleted, not
migrated.** 50 words in the `wrela build` flavour that only halt with
`EXIT_CODE_NO_RUNTIME` / an abort code. They are placeholders for a real
entry that `layout_program` has never had. Folding them into a migration
item would translate code that is about to be deleted.

**656. The cross-core quartet gets its own item.** `build_rt_xsend`,
`build_rt_xreply`, `build_rt_drain`, `build_secondary_core_entry` appear in
neither flagship golden because both are single-core, so they are easy to
forget — and leaving them would end M10 with the multi-core path still
hand-assembled, contradicting the milestone's own "no Rust-shaped hole"
claim. Item F2, after item F, pinned by the `boot-cross-core*` goldens.

**657. `build_entry_driver` is scoped explicitly, including what may not
migrate.** At 174 words it is the largest single routine in the image, and
part of it — materializing its own code address for the abort landing pad
— is floor category 4 by decision 650. Item I migrates what can migrate
and *states in the plan* what remains, so the milestone does not quietly
end with an unexamined 174-word hole.


**658. The one transcript quoting an absolute address is fixed at the
source, not granted a standing exception.** Item D cannot avoid moving
`pooldata` — it deletes 62 words from `rtcode` and adds a compiled routine
to `code` — so `err-boot-blk-cross-device-pool`'s fault message moves again,
and decision 556's pure-rebase exception is explicitly denied to migration
items. Rather than widen that exception (which would then be available
whenever it is inconvenient, i.e. always), **change the VMM's fault message
to name the pool-relative offset instead of the absolute guest address.**
The message already names the pool, and a pool-relative offset is stable
under every future layout change and strictly more useful than an absolute
address the report can already give you. For this case the offset is
**`0x0`** — the pool is one 512-byte slot and the refused range is the whole
of it — so the golden must also pin a *non-trivial* offset somewhere, or a
regression that always printed zero would pass. A unit assertion covers it
(+8 into the foreign window reads back as `offset: 8`). One reviewed transcript change now, and the
hostage is gone permanently. Its own commit, before item B. Rejected:
extending decision 556 to items B–I, which would blunt the differential
oracle exactly where it matters most.

**659. `build_rt_drain`'s argument-word count must become the same
expression as dispatch's.** `build_rt_drain` uses `(slot_size - 16) / 8`
(`layout.rs:6745`); dispatch clamps with `.min(2)` (`layout.rs:6391`). They
disagree for any actor whose declared slot is wider than two argument
words — `check-placement-cross`'s `Heavy` declares four `u64` params and
sizes its slot to 48 bytes, legal and pinned, though never called.
Unreachable today because no wide-slot actor has a cross-core edge; a live
truncation bug the first time one does. **Item F2 owns it**, and must write
the two as one expression rather than two constants that happen to agree.

**660. Item J moves before every migration item.** Its stated dependency
is item 0 alone, and it grows `TURN_RECORD_SIZE` 56→64 — which moves
`turn_stride` and every `report.txt` with turns. Doing that before B–I
means each routine is translated once, against the final turn record, and
its churn does not collide with a migration's own report churn. Rejected:
leaving J after I, which is what the original dependency line said and
which would force every migrated routine's goldens to move twice.

**661. The locked guest lane is `tests/golden/boot-actors`, not
`boot-hello`.** Measured: `bench guest` on `boot-hello` has median
~61-78ms of which ~50ms is `hv_vm_create` + 1 GiB zeroing, and the image
declares **zero actors**, so the lane cannot see a scheduler regression
at any threshold. `boot-actors` is already in the corpus, short (one
`@test(runtime)`), and scheduler-exercising: two actors, mailbox FIFO,
await park/resume, non-reentrancy (M6 item F flagship, marks=123).
Rejected: `appliance` — it is the `bench build` workload, has no
`@test(runtime)`, and cannot be timed by `bench_guest_lane`'s existing
test-image path. Rejected: `boot-actor-smoke` — one sync method on one
actor; too little scheduler path for an M10 before/after that exists to
watch the scheduler.

**662. Guest stays an absolute lock, not per-entry.** GOAL.md's
"lock microseconds per entry" known-risk was about the *compiler* lanes
(corpus-sized; already done). Guest times one fixed workload, so there
is no corpus to divide by and nothing to dilute. Inventing a per-entry
guest methodology would be cargo-culting the compiler change. The early
draft of this item's checkbox that said "re-lock on the per-entry
methodology" was wrong on that point; this decision corrects it.

**663. Pre-migration baseline and lock.** Measured on the locking machine
across five `cargo xtask bench guest` runs (2 warmup + 5 timed each) of
`boot-actors`: medians **66644 / 70885 / 70084 / 67609 / 67287us** (min
65916us, max 98364us across all timed boots). Quiet median ~67ms.
Locked at `boot_actors_median_us = 700000` — 10x quiet, rounded up to a
clean number, same rule as every other key in `bench/thresholds.toml`.
Exact counts across every timed boot of the baseline image: transcript=34
bytes, exit_code=0, exits=1, choices=0.

**664. Exact-count assertions stay mandatory across the workload
change.** Threshold alone would miss a nondeterminism regression that
happened to stay fast. `bench_guest_lane` still asserts byte-identical
transcripts and identical exit code / vCPU exits / choice-sequence count
across all timed boots of the same image — decision 14's other half,
unchanged by 661.

**665. Cross-core reply slots carry the tag in word 0's spare half.**
Item 0c1 left the upper 32 bits of the reply-ring slot's TurnId word as
unused padding. Item J packs `reply_tag` there
(`TurnId | (tag << 32)`) and keeps `REPLY_SLOT_SIZE = 16`. Rejected:
growing the slot to 24 bytes, which would resize every reply ring and
move every cross-core golden for eight bytes of tag that already fit.

**666. `Admission.Full` is opaque reason code 0.** `Admission` is still
an opaque 8-byte builtin (no source-level variants yet). Mailbox-full
`NotAdmitted` writes `0` into `OFF_TURN_REPLY` as the payload beside
`CALL_ERROR_TAG_NOT_ADMITTED` (= 3). When `Admission` grows real
variants, this is the first ordinal — matching 05-library.md §2's
declared order (`Full | Restarting | StaleRequest | DeadlineUnmeetable`).

**582. Runtime wrela source is always loaded, never imported.** The
loader is import-driven (`loader.rs:21-30`) with one precedent for
injection (`ensure_time_module`, `loader.rs:334-340`). A migrated
console/scheduler routine that user source never names would never enter
the build closure. Item A2d adds `ensure_runtime_module`, modelled on
that precedent, loading `stdlib/core/runtime.wr` into every image that
has a runtime (every `@test(runtime)` / actor / async image). Rejected:
requiring every golden to `from core.runtime import …`, which is a
corpus-wide edit for a compiler obligation.

**583. Migrated runtime fns are force-rooted into the emit set.**
`guest_reachable_keys` seeds only `@test(runtime)`, free `async` fns, and
actor/driver entry points (`lower.rs:343-376`). A fn whose only caller is
a hand-asm `Reloc::Call` is not a seed and is never lowered, so the reloc
fails with "was never codegen'd". Item A2d adds a named force-root set —
the well-known `__wrela_*` / runtime keys the harness BLs into — beside
the existing seeds. Linking itself already works (`Asm::bl_call_key`,
`layout.rs:8641`). Rejected: putting helpers on a driver/actor so they
become seeds by accident, which couples the runtime to an actor surface
it does not need.

**584. `core.runtime` is toolchain source, not user-visible API.** The
module is loaded for the image's own sake; its names are not auto-visible
in user source (same discipline as `core.time`'s non-auto-import half).
User code that wants a runtime helper imports it explicitly. That keeps
~83 boot goldens byte-identical on the load, and stops the runtime module
from becoming an accidental prelude.

**585. A2d lands after A2c and before B1.** A2c gives the placed-static
addressing the console routines need; A2d gives the load+emit path those
routines need to be *called*. Neither alone unblocks B. Sequenced as two
items rather than absorbed into B1, so a failure in either is bisectable.

**667. Auto-injected `core.runtime` is omitted from BuildInput and stage
dumps unless explicitly imported.** Decision 584 wants ~83 boot goldens
byte-identical on the load; always-loading would otherwise add
`Input path=core/runtime.wr` and `Module path=runtime` to every
runtime-bearing report/typed dump. Filtering the auto-injected module
(same shape as `core.time`'s check/typed dump-omit) is honest: the
module is toolchain source for the image's own sake, not a user input.
Explicit `from core.runtime import …` still appears. (Item A2d; spare
from orchestrator overflow 665–669 after A2c spent 586–589.)

**586. A module-level `static` is `static NAME: Type` with a required type
and no initializer; `@placed(ADDR)` is required on every `static`.**
ADDR is an integer literal (same shape as `@offset`). An unplaced static
has nowhere to live in this revision — there is no rtdata bump for them —
so it is refused by name rather than invented storage. (Item A2c.)

**587. A placed static's value is its base address (one word), exactly like
`Mmio[L]`.** Named-field scalar read/write lowers to the same
`MmioRead`/`MmioWrite` ops at the field's `@layout` offset. Whole-static
load/copy/pass, nested non-scalar field materialization, and indexing are
out of A2c (indexing is item B1). (Item A2c.)

**588. Placement against the three machine pages is verified by publishing
`PlacedStatic name= type= addr= size=` in the image report and using that
address in codegen.** Bytes are not emitted into the image blob — the VMM
already owns those pages — so neither `verify_section_sizes`' gap rule nor
a machine-revision bump fires. Duplicate ADDR is a named rejection.
(Item A2c.)

**589. `err-placed-unimplemented` is retargeted to wrong-position `@placed`
(not "no static exists").** Companion rejection goldens:
`err-placed-not-runtime` and `err-placed-duplicate-addr`. (Item A2c.)

## Known risks, carried into execution

- **`verify_section_sizes`' 8-byte gap rule** (`layout.rs:1771-1779`) is
  the concrete blocker for `@placed`, and it fires as `internal error:`,
  which the fuzz harness treats as a bug rather than an outcome. Whichever
  item first places a section must decide: steer the cursor to the placed
  address, or give the rule a placed-section exemption. Do not discover
  this inside a migration item.
- **A fixed `RTDATA_BASE` is machine-revision-visible** (`lib.rs:21`,
  clause `machine.layout.v1-constants`). If item A concludes the address
  must be a real constant, that is a machine revision bump with everything
  that entails — flag it rather than absorbing it.
- **Two independent layout-kind name tables** (`types.rs:1861-1864` and
  `declared_layout_kind` at `3822-3826`) can disagree; only the first is
  exhaustive-checked. The second silently yields `None`.
- **Four diagnostics hardcode "three kinds"** (`types.rs:1856`,
  `1868-1869`, `1921-1922`, `eval/image_checks.rs:1006-1011`), three of
  them golden-pinned. All move in item A's commit or the compiler lies
  about its own closed set.
- **`wire` is not a template.** It has no `LayoutKind::Wire` consumer
  anywhere in the compiler; `Bytes.read_wire` is documented and
  unimplemented. Model `@layout(runtime)` on `mmio`/`dma`, the two classes
  with real end-to-end paths.
- **Pre-existing finding, surfaced by item 0's design pass, deliberately
  not fixed in item 0.** `group_service_ctx` (`layout.rs:664-666`) builds
  its `turn_areas` list from actors plus free turns and **omits
  messageable-driver turn areas**, so a messageable driver's parked turn is
  never force-resumed by the deadline delivery scan. Item 0 makes this
  *visible* — the turn array enumerates driver turns — but fixing it moves
  a transcript, and item 0's entire value is being behaviour-preserving.
  **Item G owns it**, and must pin it with a golden that fails first.
- **`encode.rs` has no shifted-register add.** ROADMAP's "index → address
  becomes a single shifted-register add" is aspirational: `enc_add_reg`
  (`encode.rs:469`) is shift-0 only, so the real sequence is `lsl`+`add`,
  two words. Still no multiply, which was the point. Do not add an encoder
  form for this without a profile — the cleverness budget applies to the
  compiler too.
- **Do not align `turns_base` to the stride.** `add`-based indexing does
  not need it, and `verify_section_sizes`' 8-byte gap rule
  (`layout.rs:1771-1779`) fires as `internal error:` on any larger gap —
  which the fuzz harness treats as a bug rather than an outcome.
- **Expect the scheduler to get slower** (ROADMAP states this up front:
  bounds checks the hand-asm has none of, plus spill-everything frames
  against a scheduler that currently keeps everything in registers). Do
  not pre-optimize to avoid it. The byte-identical transcripts are what
  turn that regression into the exact before/after evidence the cleverness
  budget has never been able to obtain for this code.
